Document Checklist You Need Before Buying a Flat in Mumbai

Category: Blog   •   March 26, 2026

Finding the right flat in Mumbai often feels like the hardest part of the journey. The location fits the daily commute, the carpet area seems reasonable, and the price appears workable after months of searching. Then the paperwork begins, and suddenly a long list of unfamiliar terms appears: sale deed, OC certificate, RERA registration, society NOC.

Buyers get confused, and a question lingers constantly, ‘while buying a flat, what documents should be checked?’ or ‘what documents to check before buying under construction flat?’ For many, this is the moment when excitement turns into uncertainty. Which papers actually matter, and which ones are just formalities? 

This guide simplifies the documents to check before buying a flat in Mumbai, explaining every essential paper buyers should review before signing anything. Whether the purchase involves a brand-new project or a resale apartment, understanding these flat purchase documents helps avoid costly surprises later.

Why Checking Documents Before Buying a Flat is Non-negotiable?

Mumbai’s property market is highly regulated, yet paperwork errors still happen more often than most buyers expect. Sometimes it’s a missing approval from the municipal authority. In other cases, it’s an old mortgage that hasn’t been cleared properly.

A buyer might wonder, if a building looks complete and people are already living there, could anything still be wrong? Unfortunately, the answer is yes. A building may appear perfectly functional but still lack an Occupancy Certificate or have pending legal disputes. That’s exactly why experienced property advisors always insist on verifying the documents to be checked before buying a flat and making any payment beyond the token amount.

Proper verification protects buyers from problems such as:

  • Ownership disputes

  • Illegal construction or floor additions

  • Outstanding loans on the property

  • Missing municipal approvals

  • Difficulties during resale in the future

Taking time to review the right papers to check before buying a flat is not simply a formality. It’s one of the most important steps in the entire home-buying process.

The Essential Document Checklist Every Mumbai Flat Buyer Should Verify

Many steps go into buying real estate, like approvals from the city, checking your legal title to own the property, and filling out the registration paperwork. Below is a checklist of the various documents for flat purchase you will generally need when purchasing a new apartment or condo in Mumbai.

1. Title Deed – Proof of Ownership

The title deed confirms who legally owns the property and whether they have the right to sell it. This is often the first document a lawyer reviews during due diligence.

Buyers should ensure:

  • The seller’s name matches the title document

  • Ownership history is clear

  • There are no disputes linked to the property

A clear title is arguably the most important item among the documents to check before buying a flat in Mumbai, because every other agreement depends on it.

2. Agreement for Sale

The Agreement for Sale outlines the exact terms of the property transaction. It includes the agreed price, payment schedule, carpet area, possession date, and any penalties for delay.

Buyers should read this document carefully, particularly sections related to:

  • Payment milestones

  • Possession timeline

  • Additional charges such as parking or maintenance

In Mumbai, this agreement must be registered and stamped as per Maharashtra government rules.

3. RERA Registration Certificate

All new residential projects must be registered with MahaRERA. The Real Estate Regulation Act introduced greater transparency for buyers.

  • Before purchasing a new flat, buyers should confirm:

  • The project is listed on the MahaRERA website

  • Construction timelines are clearly mentioned

  • Carpet area matches the RERA disclosure

Checking the RERA certificate is now considered one of the most critical documents before buying a flat in under-construction developments.

4. Commencement Certificate (CC)

The Commencement Certificate shows that the local government has given the green light for the building to be built according to the plans that were approved. Construction shouldn't start without a valid CC.

This paper proves that:

  • Plans for building are approved.

  • It is legal to build.

  • Development follows the rules set by the city.

This paper is an important part of the flat purchase documents checklist, but buyers often forget about it.

5. Occupancy Certificate (OC)

The Occupancy Certificate shows that all construction of the building has been properly completed as per approved plans and therefore certified safe to live in. If an Occupancy Certificate has not been issued for a specific building, then there may be issues related to the building's:

  • Water and Power Utilities

  • Selling Your Property

  • Loan Approval by a Bank

When looking at ready-to-move homes, OC is one of the papers to check before buying a flat

6. Encumbrance Certificate

An Encumbrance Certificate shows whether the property has any outstanding loans or legal claims.

It confirms that:

  • The property is free from financial liabilities

  • No lender holds a claim over it

  • Ownership transfer can happen smoothly

Buyers often request this certificate covering at least the last 12–15 years of ownership history.

7. Approved Building Plan

Municipal authorities approve a building plan before construction begins. This plan specifies:

  • Floor layout

  • Number of floors permitted

  • Total buildable area

Comparing the approved plan with the actual building helps ensure there are no unauthorized changes. In dense cities like Mumbai, this verification is especially important.

8. Property Tax Receipts

Property tax payments confirm that there are no outstanding dues linked to the flat or building. Before finalising the deal, buyers should check the latest tax receipts from the municipal authority. This ensures:

  • No pending tax liabilities

  • Smooth transfer of ownership

  • Clear municipal records

9. Society No-Objection Certificate (for resale flats)

When purchasing a resale flat in a cooperative housing society, a No-Objection Certificate from the society is usually required.

This document confirms:

  • The society approves the transfer

  • The seller has no outstanding maintenance dues

  • Membership can be transferred to the new buyer

Among the documents required for flat purchase, this step is particularly important for resale properties.

10. Sale Deed

The Sale Deed is the final legal document that transfers ownership from seller to buyer. Once registered with the Sub-Registrar’s office, the buyer officially becomes the legal owner of the property.

Key details included in this document:

  • Final purchase price

  • Property description

  • Buyer and seller details

  • Registration details

Without the Sale Deed registration, ownership legally remains incomplete.

A Practical Step Many Buyers Overlook

Experienced consultants often notice one common mistake among first-time buyers. Many people verify documents only after paying a significant booking amount.

A better approach is to request copies of the major documents before buying a flat, even before signing the booking form. Most reputable property developers and sellers are comfortable sharing these records early in the process.This simple step can save weeks of stress later.

Exploring Well-documented Projects Makes the Process Easier

Established developers frequently keep structured documentation and approvals as part of their process from the early days of development. This helps buyers when they are evaluating residences in Mumbai as the verification process will go much smoother through these processes.

Ruparel’s residential developments take into account the regulatory framework from the time of planning through the provision of access to purchasers of flats with respect to providing access to flat purchase documents and approvals associated with the project throughout the purchasing process.

Taking time to review flat purchase documents and other documentation associated with purchasing flats in Mumbai will provide assurance that your purchase will be protected and is legally secure.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What are the most important documents to check before buying a flat in Mumbai?

The most important documents required for buying a flat are the Title Deed, the Agreement for Sale, the RERA registration certificate, the Occupancy Certificate, the Commencement Certificate, and the Encumbrance Certificate.

2. Is RERA registration mandatory for new projects?

Yes. MahaRERA must register all new homes that are being built. Before making a reservation, buyers should check the project details on the official RERA website.

3. Why is an Occupancy Certificate important?

The Occupancy Certificate confirms that the building complies with approved construction plans and is safe for residents. Without it, utilities and resale processes may face complications.

4. Do resale flats require different documents?

Yes. In addition to ownership documents, resale flats typically require society NOC, maintenance clearance, and previous property tax receipts.

5. Should a lawyer verify property documents before buying?

Yes, it is highly advisable. A property lawyer can examine the papers to check before buying a flat and identify potential legal issues that may not be obvious to buyers.
